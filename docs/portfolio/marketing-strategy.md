# Portfolio — marketing strategy

Internal positioning for the freelance landing page. Page structure is in [requirements.md](./requirements.md); this file is **who it is for, what to claim, and what not to look like**.

## Positioning

Jake Robins is a **web-centric freelance developer who also thinks like an operator**. Six years building software, plus twelve years in retail operations and business management. The unusual combination is fluency on **both sides of the table**: the business problem and the technical solution.

Most freelance developers optimize for the technical craft. Jake can own **product**, not only tickets: priorities, tradeoffs, and implementation. That is the core differentiator.

**One-line:** End-to-end technical partner for companies with real products—not a vendor for a one-page brochure.

## Audience

**In**

- Larger companies (or teams inside them) with **substantial products**: multiple components that have to work together.
- People hiring for **serious web product work**, not a leaflet site.
- Organizations that want someone who can move from prototype to production quickly, including **AI in the workflow and in the product**.
- Smaller businesses that need **technical leadership** without a full-time CTO budget (**fractional CTO** / product ownership).

**Out**

- Mom-and-pop brochure work (restaurant menus, simple “we exist” pages, tiny marketing sites with no product).
- Positioning that reads as a generic landing-page freelancer.

The page should make the **scale and complexity** of the intended work obvious so the wrong leads self-select out.

## Value propositions (use these on the page)

These map onto the design-brief sections: **What I take on** (2, 4–7), **Why Jake** (1, 7), **How I work** (3, 4). Exact copy TBD; claims should stay faithful to this list.

1. **Business + engineering.** Rare among freelance developers. Can talk to stakeholders and still ship.
2. **Web products, especially larger and more technical ones.** Strong at building websites and web systems that are more than a single marketing URL.
3. **Speed with AI in the workflow.** AI is used throughout how Jake works, so prototypes and final implementation can move faster for the client.
4. **AI-powered products.** Experience **implementing** AI in products, not only using ChatGPT as a coding assistant. Good fit when the client’s use case benefits from AI in the thing being built.
5. **Automation.** Workflows and products that cut manual work and save time.
6. **Fractional CTO.** Product ownership and technical direction, not only implementation—especially where a full-time CTO is not in the budget.
7. **End-to-end.** Broad enough to be useful on almost any problem that is **solvable with technology**, from understanding the business through delivery.

## What the site should emphasize

- Complexity, systems, and product—not “pretty landing pages.”
- Partnership and ownership, not staff-aug “ticket closer” (unless that is a deliberate secondary offer later).
- AI as **leverage** (speed + product capability), not as a buzzword strip.
- Evidence later should support **this** audience: three client testimonials (logo + title + quote), not a grab-bag of small local businesses.

## What the site should not emphasize

- Lowest-price commodity web design.
- A vibe aimed at tiny local retail with no product.
- Pure “I only write code you specify” with no product judgment.

## Inflows and message

The URL will be passed around. Every section should still make sense to a cold visitor from search or a referral. Lead with **who it’s for** and **what kind of work**, then proof and contact.

Contact remains **email only** ([objectives.md](./objectives.md)).
